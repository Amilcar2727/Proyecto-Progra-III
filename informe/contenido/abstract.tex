\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

This work presents an optimal route management system for parcel delivery in the city of Cusco, implemented as a Python desktop application with a Tkinter-based graphical user interface. The core problem consists of efficiently determining which delivery orders a courier should fulfill and the sequence in which to visit them, minimizing total travel distance while respecting vehicle capacity constraints and the existence of blocked streets in the road graph. To solve it, four algorithmic paradigms were implemented from scratch without external optimization libraries: (1) a \textit{Greedy} nearest-neighbor heuristic for route construction; (2) \textit{Merge Sort} (Divide and Conquer) for order sorting; (3) \textit{0/1 Knapsack} and \textit{Held-Karp} (Dynamic Programming) for optimal order selection and exact TSP routing; and (4) \textit{Backtracking} with cycle pruning for alternative route exploration when streets are blocked. Empirical benchmarks confirmed the theoretical complexity predictions: Held-Karp reached 42.2 ms for $n=12$ orders with a Greedy speedup of $138.8\times$, while the Greedy suboptimality gap grew from 0\% at $n=3$ to 14.81\% at $n=12$. The Backtracking algorithm found between 3 and 9 alternative routes in less than 1.1 ms across four Cusco graph scenarios. The resulting application is stable, visually verifiable, and allows all test scenarios to be reproduced interactively.

\textit{Keywords:} optimal routes, greedy algorithms, dynamic programming, backtracking, Held-Karp, Cusco, TSP, Knapsack
